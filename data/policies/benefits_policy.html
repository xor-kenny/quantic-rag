Benefits Eligibility Policy

Purpose and Scope

This policy explains who is eligible for benefits at Northwind Systems, a fictional company used for coursework, when eligibility begins, how to enrol, and when elections may be changed. It applies to employees working in the United States. Where a plan document and this summary disagree, the plan document controls.

Nothing in this policy is a promise of coverage. Eligibility is determined by the plan documents and by applicable law, and the company may amend or discontinue a plan at any time.

Eligibility

Regular full-time employees scheduled for at least 30 hours per week are eligible for the company medical, dental, vision, and retirement plans after their eligibility date. Temporary and part-time employees are not eligible unless a plan document or applicable law states otherwise.

The thirty-hour threshold is measured against the employee's regularly scheduled hours as recorded in the employee record, not against hours actually worked in a particular week. An employee scheduled for twenty-five hours who works thirty-five hours in a busy week does not become eligible.

The eligibility date is the first day of the month following the start date. An employee starting on the first day of a month is eligible on that day.

An employee whose scheduled hours increase to thirty or more becomes eligible on the first of the month following the change. An employee whose hours decrease below thirty loses eligibility at the end of the month in which the change takes effect, and is offered continuation coverage where the law requires it.

Two plan features are deliberately broader than the general eligibility rule. The 401(k) plan is open to all employees aged twenty-one or over regardless of scheduled hours, subject to the plan's service requirement. The employee assistance programme is available to every employee and to members of their household at no cost, including part-time employees who are not eligible for medical coverage.

Enrollment and Changes

Newly eligible employees have 30 days from their eligibility notice to enroll. Mid-year changes require a qualifying life event and supporting documentation within 30 days. Benefits questions involving plan interpretation should be escalated to the Benefits team.

An employee who does not enrol within the thirty-day window is treated as having declined coverage for the plan year, except for any plan with automatic enrolment. The next opportunity is the annual open enrolment period unless a qualifying life event occurs.

Open enrolment is held each autumn for coverage effective the following January. Elections made during open enrolment cannot be changed during the plan year without a qualifying life event.

Qualifying Life Events

A qualifying life event allows a mid-year election change consistent with the event. Recognised events include marriage, divorce or legal separation, birth or adoption of a child, death of a dependent, a change in the employee's or a spouse's employment status affecting benefits eligibility, a dependent gaining or losing eligibility, and a court order requiring coverage of a dependent.

The election change must be requested within thirty days of the event and must be consistent with it. Adding a newborn to medical coverage is consistent with a birth; switching from Medical Silver to Medical Gold at the same time generally is not.

Supporting documentation is required in every case. A marriage certificate, birth certificate, or letter from another employer confirming a coverage change are typical examples. The Benefits team confirms what is required for a given event.

Retirement Plan

Employees may elect a salary deferral into the 401(k) plan up to the annual limit set by law. Deferral elections may be changed at any time and take effect with the next practicable payroll run.

The company match, the vesting schedule, and the investment options are described in the summary plan description. Employees should consult that document and, where appropriate, their own financial adviser. The Benefits team can explain how the plan works but cannot give investment advice.

Leave and Coverage Continuation

Coverage generally continues during an approved paid leave on the same terms as active employment. During an unpaid leave, the employee remains responsible for their share of premiums; the Benefits team arranges collection.

Where employment ends, or where an employee loses eligibility because their hours were reduced, continuation coverage is offered as required by law. The election notice is sent to the address on file, so employees should keep that address current.

Common Questions

I am part-time. Am I eligible for medical benefits? No. Regular full-time employees scheduled for at least thirty hours per week are eligible; temporary and part-time employees are not, unless a plan document or applicable law provides otherwise. You may still participate in the 401(k) plan if you are aged twenty-one or over, and the employee assistance programme is available to you at no cost.

My hours are increasing to thirty-five per week. You become eligible on the first of the month following the change, and you will receive an eligibility notice starting a thirty-day enrolment window.

I had a baby last week. Can I add them? Yes. Birth is a qualifying life event. Request the change within thirty days and provide the supporting documentation the Benefits team asks for.

I want to switch plans because my doctor left the network. A network change is not by itself a qualifying life event. Raise it with the Benefits team, who will confirm whether any plan-specific provision applies; otherwise the change waits for open enrolment.

Is my question about what a plan covers answered here? No. Questions involving plan interpretation, such as whether a specific treatment is covered, should be escalated to the Benefits team rather than answered from this summary.

Exceptions and Interpretation

The Benefits team owns this policy and is the only authorised interpreter of it. Eligibility rules are set by plan documents and by law and are not subject to managerial discretion or local exception.

# Health, Safety and Wellbeing Policy

## Purpose and Scope

This policy sets out how Northwind Systems, a fictional company used for coursework, protects the physical and mental health of its people. It applies to employees, contractors, and visitors at company offices, and to employees working from an approved remote location.

Safety obligations do not stop at the office door. An employee working from home is working in a company workplace for the purposes of this policy, and the company's duty of care applies to them in the same way.

## Office Safety

Each office has a named safety coordinator, a posted evacuation plan, and a designated assembly point. New employees are shown these during their first week; visitors are briefed at reception.

Fire drills are held twice per year at each office. Participation is mandatory for everyone present, including visitors and employees attending the office by exception that day.

Employees must not prop open fire doors, obstruct exits or extinguishers, or override door controls. Anything that blocks an exit route should be reported to the safety coordinator immediately rather than moved by the person who found it.

Trained first aiders are identified on the notice board at each office and in the self-service portal. First aid kits and defibrillators are located at reception and in each kitchen area.

## Reporting Incidents and Hazards

Any injury, near miss, or hazard must be reported to the safety coordinator and logged in the incident system, however minor it appears. A near miss that caused no harm this time is the most useful report the company receives, because it identifies a risk before someone is hurt.

Reports are logged without blame. No employee will face disciplinary action for reporting an incident in good faith, including one they caused themselves.

Serious incidents, meaning any injury requiring treatment beyond on-site first aid, must additionally be reported to People Operations within twenty-four hours so that any statutory reporting obligation can be met.

Where an injury occurs at an employee's remote work location during working hours, it should be reported in the same way. Employees are often reluctant to report these; they should not be.

## Remote Workstation Safety

Employees working remotely are responsible for maintaining a safe work area: adequate lighting, a stable chair and work surface, clear walkways, and no trailing cables across a route.

An ergonomic assessment is available to every employee on request, conducted remotely for those not office-based. Equipment identified as necessary by an assessment is provided at company expense; see the Equipment and Asset Policy.

Employees should take regular breaks from screen work. The company does not monitor break-taking, and no employee should feel that stepping away from a screen requires justification.

## Mental Health and Wellbeing

The employee assistance programme provides confidential counselling, financial guidance, and legal referral at no cost to every employee and to members of their household, including part-time employees. Use of the programme is confidential and is not reported to the company in any form that identifies an individual.

Mental health is treated as health. An absence for a mental health reason is treated exactly as an absence for a physical health reason, under the Paid Time Off Policy for a short absence and the Leave of Absence Policy for a longer one.

Managers who become aware that an employee is struggling should focus on what practical support the company can offer rather than on diagnosis. Managers must not ask for medical detail, must not record what they are told, and must not take it into account in any performance assessment.

An employee who needs an adjustment to their role, schedule, or environment for a health reason should contact People Operations, which handles accommodation requests confidentially and does not disclose the underlying reason to the manager.

## Workload and Working Hours

Sustained excessive workload is a safety matter, not only a productivity one. Managers are expected to monitor workload and to escalate where a team cannot deliver what is being asked within reasonable hours.

Employees are not expected to respond to messages outside their working hours. A message sent outside those hours does not carry an expectation of an immediate reply, and managers should say so explicitly rather than assume it is understood.

Where an on-call rotation regularly disturbs rest, the manager should review the rotation with the team rather than treat it as an individual issue.

## Illness at Work

An employee who becomes unwell at the office should tell their manager or the safety coordinator and go home. Employees should not attend the office with a contagious illness, and no employee should feel pressure to do so.

Where an employee is well enough to work but should not be in a shared space, they may work remotely for the duration with their manager's agreement, without this counting against any remote-work expectation their role carries.

## Substance Use

Employees must not be impaired by alcohol or drugs while working, on company premises, or while representing the company. This applies equally to remote work.

Alcohol served at a company event is subject to the same expectation of professional conduct as any other work setting, and the Workplace Conduct and Reporting Policy applies in full. Non-alcoholic options are provided at every company event.

An employee with a substance dependency is encouraged to seek support through the employee assistance programme. An employee who comes forward voluntarily before a conduct issue arises will be supported through treatment and will not face disciplinary action for having sought help.

Prescription medication that may affect the ability to work safely should be discussed with People Operations, not the manager, so that any necessary adjustment can be made confidentially.

## Violence and Personal Safety

Threats, intimidation, and violence are prohibited and are handled under the Workplace Conduct and Reporting Policy. Where there is an immediate risk to physical safety, contact local emergency services first and Security as soon as it is safe.

Employees who receive a threat connected to their work, including online, should report it to Security. The company will support an employee facing a personal safety concern that affects their work, including through schedule or location adjustments arranged confidentially.

Lone working at an office outside normal hours requires the employee to sign in through the building system so that their presence is known.

## Common Questions

**I tripped over a cable at home during work hours. Do I report it?** Yes. Remote work locations are covered by this policy. Report it in the incident system.

**Is the employee assistance programme really confidential?** Yes. Usage is not reported to the company in any way that identifies you, and it is available at no cost to you and your household.

**I need a day off for my mental health.** Record it as PTO in the ordinary way. You do not need to give a reason. If you need more than two consecutive days, contact People Operations, because a longer absence may qualify as a leave.

**My manager keeps messaging me at 10pm.** You are not expected to respond outside your working hours. If the pattern continues, raise it with your manager or with People Operations.

**Do I have to attend the fire drill if I only came in for one meeting?** Yes. Participation is mandatory for everyone present.

## Exceptions and Interpretation

Workplace Services owns the office safety provisions; People Operations owns the wellbeing, accommodation, and workload provisions. The confidentiality of employee assistance programme use and of accommodation requests is not subject to exception.

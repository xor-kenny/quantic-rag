# Performance and Development Policy

## Purpose and Scope

This policy describes how performance is discussed, reviewed, and supported at Northwind Systems, a fictional company used for coursework. It applies to all regular employees and to the managers responsible for them.

The company's approach rests on a simple premise: an employee should never be surprised by a performance review. Anything that appears in a written review should already have been said in a one-to-one conversation.

## Ongoing Conversations

Managers hold a one-to-one meeting with each direct report at least every two weeks. The meeting belongs to the employee, and its agenda is theirs to set.

Feedback is expected to be timely and specific. A manager who observes something worth correcting should raise it within days, not save it for a review cycle. The same applies to positive feedback.

Employees are encouraged to request feedback rather than waiting for it, and to ask for specifics where feedback is too general to act on.

## Review Cycle

Formal reviews are held twice per year, in June and December. Each cycle covers the preceding six months.

| Stage | Owner | Timing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Self-assessment | Employee | Weeks 1–2 of cycle |
| Peer feedback requests | Employee, manager | Weeks 1–2 |
| Peer feedback submission | Nominated peers | Weeks 2–3 |
| Manager assessment | Manager | Weeks 3–4 |
| Calibration | Manager group, People Operations | Week 5 |
| Review conversation | Manager, employee | Week 6 |
| Compensation outcome | People Operations | Week 8, December cycle only |

Employees nominate three to five peers for feedback. Managers may add nominations where an employee's work spans teams. Peer feedback is shared with the employee in summarised form, attributed only where the peer agrees.

Calibration exists to make ratings consistent across teams. It compares how managers have applied the rating definitions, and it may adjust a rating. A manager whose rating is adjusted in calibration must be able to explain the final rating to the employee in their own words.

## Rating Definitions

| Rating | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Exceptional | Consistently delivered beyond the scope and expectations of the role |
| Strong | Delivered fully against the expectations of the role |
| Developing | Delivered against some expectations; specific gaps identified |
| Not meeting | Did not deliver against the expectations of the role |

Most employees performing their role well receive Strong. Strong is a good outcome and should be communicated as one. Exceptional describes a genuinely unusual contribution, not a good year.

Developing is common and expected for employees new to a role or recently promoted. It is not a warning; it identifies where support is needed.

There is no forced distribution. Managers are not required to place a set proportion of their team in any rating.

## Goals

Employees set three to five goals per cycle with their manager. Goals state an outcome rather than an activity, and are written so that both parties can tell at the end of the cycle whether they were met.

Goals may be revised mid-cycle where priorities change. A goal that became irrelevant through a change of business direction is not held against the employee.

Development goals are distinct from delivery goals. Each employee should carry at least one development goal per cycle, and managers should protect time for it.

## Development and Learning

Every employee has an annual learning budget, published by People Operations, covering courses, conferences, books, and certifications. Requests follow the Business Expense and Equipment Policy and require manager pre-approval.

Conference attendance also requires travel approval; see the Business Travel Policy. Employees presenting at an external conference on the company's behalf have their attendance funded outside the personal learning budget.

Internal mentoring is available to any employee on request. Mentoring pairs are arranged by People Operations and are outside the reporting line, so that conversations can be candid.

Employees interested in a different role should raise it with their manager or, where they prefer, directly with People Operations. Interest in an internal move is not treated as disloyalty and must not affect an employee's rating or assignments.

## Promotion

Promotion recognises that an employee is already operating at the next level, not that they are ready to try it. Managers build a case with evidence from the preceding two cycles and submit it during the review cycle.

Promotion decisions are made in a cross-team committee rather than by a single manager, to keep the bar consistent. Outcomes are communicated in the review conversation.

An unsuccessful promotion case should result in specific, written guidance on what is missing. A manager who cannot articulate what is missing should not have submitted the case.

## Performance Improvement

Where performance is not meeting the expectations of the role, the manager and People Operations agree a performance improvement plan. The plan states the specific gaps, the expected standard, the support the company will provide, and a review date, normally sixty days out.

A plan is not a formality preceding termination. Employees do complete plans successfully, and the plan should be written so that success is genuinely achievable.

Employees on a plan may bring a People Operations representative to any meeting about it. Employees who believe a plan was issued unfairly, or in response to a protected activity, should raise it under the Workplace Conduct and Reporting Policy.

Where performance does not improve by the review date, outcomes may include an extension, a role change, or termination. That decision requires People Operations approval and may not be made by the manager alone.

## Confidentiality

Ratings, review documents, and improvement plans are confidential to the employee, their management line, and People Operations. Managers must not discuss an employee's rating with other employees, including in a calibration context outside the calibration meeting itself.

Medical information, leave records, and accommodation requests must play no part in a performance assessment. A manager who becomes aware of such information incidentally must exclude it from their assessment; see the Leave of Absence Policy.

An employee's absence on an approved leave must not reduce their rating. Where a leave shortened the period under review, the assessment covers the period actually worked.

## Common Questions

**I got Strong and expected Exceptional.** Ask your manager for the specific evidence that would have supported Exceptional. Strong means you delivered fully against your role; it is the expected outcome for someone performing well.

**Can my rating change after my manager tells me?** The rating is finalised in calibration before the review conversation, so the rating you are told is final for that cycle.

**I was on parental leave for three months. How is my review handled?** The assessment covers the period you actually worked. Your leave must not reduce your rating.

**Does a performance improvement plan mean I am being managed out?** No. Plans are written to be achievable and employees do complete them. If you believe the plan is unfair, raise it with People Operations.

**Can I use my learning budget for a conference abroad?** The registration comes from your learning budget with manager pre-approval, and the travel requires separate approval under the Business Travel Policy.

## Exceptions and Interpretation

People Operations owns this policy. Deviations from the review timetable require People Operations agreement. The confidentiality provisions and the prohibition on considering leave or medical information are not subject to exception.

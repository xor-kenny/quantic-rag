# Remote Work and Location Policy

## Purpose and Scope

This policy governs where employees of Northwind Systems, a fictional company used for coursework, are permitted to perform their work. It applies to all employees regardless of whether their role is designated office-based, hybrid, or remote. It covers both permanent work locations and temporary arrangements such as working from a relative's home during a holiday period.

Where an employee works is not solely a matter of personal preference. The company incurs payroll registration, tax, insurance, immigration, export-control, and customer-contract obligations based on the physical location from which work is performed. Those obligations are the reason approvals exist.

## Approved Work Locations

Employees may work from their approved home location or assigned office. A temporary work location in another US state requires manager and People Operations approval before work begins when it lasts more than ten business days.

Each employee has exactly one approved home location on file. It is recorded during onboarding and appears in the employee self-service record. Employees who move must update People Operations at least thirty days before the move, because a change of work state may change payroll registration, benefits plan availability, and paid-leave entitlements.

Working from a coffee shop, coworking space, or library within the employee's approved metropolitan area does not require additional approval, but does require compliance with the security requirements in this policy.

## Domestic Temporary Work Locations

A stay of ten business days or fewer in another US state requires no formal approval, but the employee should tell their manager in advance so that coverage and time zone expectations are clear.

A stay longer than ten business days requires approval from both the manager and People Operations before work begins. People Operations reviews whether the destination state creates a payroll registration obligation, whether the company already has a registered presence there, and whether the employee's role has any location-restricted access.

Certain states require registration after a single day of work performed within their borders. People Operations maintains the current list. Because that list changes, employees should request review rather than assume that a short stay is exempt.

Approval for a temporary domestic location is granted for a stated period with a stated end date. It does not change the employee's approved home location, and it does not renew automatically.

## International Work

Working outside the United States requires written approval from People Operations, Security, Payroll, and the employee's vice president before travel. Approval is not guaranteed because immigration, payroll, tax, export-control, and customer obligations may apply. Employees must not work internationally until approvals are documented.

All four approvals are required. A partial approval set is not sufficient, and no single approver can waive the others. Requests should be submitted at least six weeks before the intended departure date, because immigration and tax review cannot be completed quickly.

Approval is more likely for short stays in countries where Northwind Systems already has a registered entity, and considerably less likely for stays that approach or exceed the period after which a country asserts tax residency. Many countries treat a stay of more than thirty days as creating obligations, and a stay approaching or exceeding six weeks is very likely to require formal review of permanent-establishment risk.

Employees holding a visa or work authorization tied to their presence in the United States must consult Immigration Support before requesting international work, because time spent outside the country can affect status.

Certain roles are ineligible for international work regardless of duration. These include roles with access to export-controlled technical data and roles subject to a customer contract that restricts data access by geography. Security identifies these roles during the approval review.

## Security Requirements

Remote workers must use company-managed equipment, full-disk encryption, multi-factor authentication, and the approved VPN for company systems. Public Wi-Fi requires the VPN; confidential information must not be displayed or discussed where others can see or hear it.

Personal devices may not be used to access company systems, with the single exception of the approved authenticator application on a personal phone. Company data may not be copied to personal cloud storage, personal email, or removable media.

Employees working from a shared or public space must use a privacy screen when handling confidential information and must not join a video call in which confidential material is displayed unless they are using headphones in a location where the screen is not visible to others.

Devices must not be left unattended in a vehicle or a public space. A lost or stolen device must be reported to Security immediately, and in any event within twenty-four hours; see the Data Security Policy for the reporting procedure.

## Equipment and Home Office

The company issues a laptop, and where the role requires it, a monitor and docking station. Employees working remotely more than three days per week may request a home office stipend once per three-year period; the current amount is published by Workplace Services.

Requests for a home office chair or accessory follow the Business Expense and Equipment Policy and require manager pre-approval and a documented business need before purchase. Reimbursement is not available for a purchase made before approval.

Internet service at the employee's approved home location is the employee's responsibility except where state law requires reimbursement. Employees in those states should contact Payroll.

## Working Hours and Availability

Remote employees are expected to be available during their team's published core hours and to maintain a calendar that accurately reflects their working schedule and time zone.

An employee working from a temporary location in a different time zone should agree expectations with their manager in advance. Approval for a temporary location does not entitle the employee to change their core hours, and it does not relieve them of scheduled meetings.

Employees must not perform work while in transit through a country for which they lack approval, including work performed during a layover.

## Common Questions

**Can I work from another country for six weeks?** Not without written approval from People Operations, Security, Payroll, and your vice president, documented before you travel. Six weeks is long enough to raise tax and permanent-establishment questions, so the review is substantive rather than a formality. Start the request at least six weeks ahead.

**I am visiting family in another state for two weeks. Do I need approval?** Yes. Two weeks exceeds ten business days, so manager and People Operations approval is required before work begins.

**My spouse is relocating and I want to move permanently.** Tell People Operations at least thirty days before the move. Your benefits, payroll withholding, and paid-leave entitlements may change, and some roles cannot be performed from every state.

**Can I use hotel Wi-Fi?** Yes, with the approved VPN active. Without the VPN you may not access company systems.

## Exceptions and Interpretation

People Operations owns this policy and coordinates approvals across Security, Payroll, and Immigration Support. Exceptions require the same approvals as the underlying request and are granted in writing for a stated period.

Working from an unapproved location is a policy violation and may result in disciplinary action, and may also create personal tax liability for the employee. Employees who realize they have worked from an unapproved location should disclose it promptly; disclosure is treated far more favorably than discovery.

# Workplace Conduct and Reporting Policy

## Purpose and Scope

This policy sets the standard of conduct expected at Northwind Systems, a fictional company used for coursework, and explains how a concern is raised and handled. It applies to employees, contractors, interns, and vendors while they are engaged in company business, in any location, including remote settings, company events, customer sites, and work-related messaging channels.

The policy is deliberately broad about where it applies. Conduct in a chat channel at 9pm and conduct in a conference room at 9am are treated the same way.

## Reporting Concerns

The company prohibits harassment, discrimination, retaliation, violence, and threats. Employees may report a concern to People Operations, a manager, or the confidential reporting channel. Reports are handled as privately as practical and are reviewed promptly.

There is no requirement to raise a concern with a manager first. An employee may go directly to People Operations or the confidential reporting channel, and may do so anonymously. An employee who is uncomfortable reporting internally may contact the external ethics line published in the handbook.

A report does not need to allege a policy violation with legal precision. Describing what happened, when, and who was present is enough. It is the reviewer's job, not the reporter's, to determine which policy applies.

## What Counts as Prohibited Conduct

Harassment is unwelcome conduct based on a protected characteristic that is severe or pervasive enough to create an intimidating, hostile, or offensive working environment. Protected characteristics include race, colour, religion, national origin, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, age, disability, pregnancy, veteran status, genetic information, and any other characteristic protected by applicable law.

Discrimination is an adverse employment decision made because of a protected characteristic. Adverse decisions include hiring, assignment, promotion, compensation, discipline, and termination.

Retaliation is an adverse action taken because someone made a good-faith report, participated in a review, or refused to take part in conduct they reasonably believed was unlawful. Retaliation is prohibited even where the underlying report is not substantiated.

Bullying and abusive conduct are prohibited whether or not they are tied to a protected characteristic. Repeated belittling, deliberate exclusion from information needed to do a job, and sustained verbal aggression fall within this policy.

## How a Report Is Handled

People Operations acknowledges a report within two business days. An intake conversation is scheduled to understand what happened and what the reporter wants to happen next.

Where a review is warranted, People Operations assigns a trained reviewer who has no reporting relationship to anyone involved. The reviewer gathers relevant documents, interviews the people identified, and prepares written findings.

Reviews are handled as privately as practical, but they are not confidential in the absolute sense. Information is shared with those who need it to conduct the review or to act on its outcome. No one conducting a review may promise complete confidentiality, because doing so would prevent the company from acting.

Both the person who raised the concern and the person whose conduct is under review are told the outcome at the level of detail appropriate to their role in it.

## Non-Retaliation and Emergencies

Retaliation for making a good-faith report or participating in a review is prohibited. For immediate danger, contact local emergency services first. The agent must not investigate, make findings, or promise confidentiality; it should route the issue to trained HR staff.

Any employee who believes they have experienced retaliation should report it as a new concern. Retaliation reports are prioritised and are reviewed by someone independent of the original review.

Where there is an immediate risk to physical safety, contact local emergency services before contacting the company. Company Security should be notified as soon as it is safe to do so.

## Limits on Automated and Informal Handling

Automated assistants, chatbots, and self-service tools must not attempt to assess, investigate, or resolve a conduct concern. Their only appropriate action is to route the person to People Operations or the confidential reporting channel, and to say plainly that a trained person will follow up.

These tools must not ask the reporter for a detailed account of the incident, must not characterise conduct as a violation or not a violation, must not offer a view on likely outcomes, and must not promise confidentiality.

The same limits apply to managers who receive a report informally. A manager's obligation on hearing a concern is to route it to People Operations promptly. Managers may not conduct their own investigation, may not attempt to mediate between the parties, and may not decide that a concern is too minor to pass on.

## Obligations of Managers

Managers who receive a report, or who observe conduct that may violate this policy, must notify People Operations promptly even if the person raising it asks them not to. This obligation is not discretionary.

Managers must not take any adverse action against a person who has raised a concern, and must consult People Operations before making any change to that person's role, assignment, or schedule while a review is open.

## Consequences

Substantiated violations result in corrective action proportionate to the conduct, up to and including termination. Factors include the severity of the conduct, whether it was repeated, the seniority of the person involved, and whether there was a prior warning.

Knowingly making a false report is itself a violation. A report that is not substantiated is not a false report; the distinction is whether the person knew the allegation was untrue when they made it.

## Common Questions

**I do not want an investigation, I just want it to stop.** Say so during intake. People Operations will explain the options, which may include an informal resolution. Some conduct is serious enough that the company must review it regardless of the reporter's preference, and People Operations will tell you if that applies.

**Can I report anonymously?** Yes, through the confidential reporting channel. Be aware that anonymity can limit how far a review can go, because the reviewer may be unable to ask follow-up questions.

**My manager is the problem.** Report to People Operations or the confidential reporting channel directly. You are not required to raise a concern with the person it concerns.

**Will my manager be told I reported something?** Only if their involvement is necessary, and never as a matter of routine. Tell the intake reviewer if you have a concern about who is informed.

## Exceptions and Interpretation

People Operations owns this policy. It is not subject to exception, and no manager, however senior, may waive any part of it. Questions about interpretation should be directed to People Operations rather than resolved locally.

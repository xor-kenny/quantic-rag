# Business Expense and Equipment Policy

## Purpose and Scope

This policy explains which business expenses Northwind Systems, a fictional company used for coursework, will reimburse, what documentation is required, and how approval works. It applies to all employees and to any contractor whose agreement states that expenses are reimbursable.

The governing principle is that the company reimburses costs that are reasonable, necessary, and incurred for a business purpose. An expense that is merely convenient, or that primarily benefits the employee personally, is not reimbursable even if it is modest in amount.

## Allowable Expenses

Reasonable, necessary, and documented business expenses may be reimbursed when pre-approved where required. Itemized receipts are required for expenses of $25 or more. Alcohol, personal upgrades, and ordinary commuting costs are not reimbursable.

An itemized receipt shows the vendor, the date, the individual items purchased, and the total. A credit card slip showing only a total is not an itemized receipt and will be returned. Where a vendor cannot provide an itemized receipt, the employee should attach a written explanation and their manager's confirmation.

Expenses must be submitted within sixty days of being incurred. Submissions after ninety days require a written exception from Finance and may be treated as taxable income to the employee.

## Home Office and Equipment

Company-issued equipment is the standard for work. A home office chair or accessory requires manager pre-approval and a documented business need before purchase. Employees must use the approved purchasing process for laptops and other computing devices; personal laptops are not reimbursable without a written exception from IT and Finance.

The reason computing devices are treated differently from furniture is security rather than cost. A company-managed laptop carries disk encryption, endpoint monitoring, and a managed software inventory. A personal laptop does not, and cannot be brought into compliance retroactively by reimbursing its purchase price.

Where an employee has a documented accessibility need, IT and Workplace Services will source appropriate equipment directly rather than reimbursing a personal purchase. Employees should raise these needs with People Operations, which coordinates the request confidentially.

Equipment purchased with company funds remains company property and must be returned on separation. See the Equipment and Asset Policy for the return procedure.

## Pre-Approval

Pre-approval is required for any single expense above $500, for all travel, for conference registration, and for any home office purchase. Pre-approval is requested in the expense system and must be granted before the commitment is made.

An expense incurred without required pre-approval may still be reimbursed at Finance's discretion where the business need is clear and the omission was inadvertent. Repeated omissions are treated as a compliance matter rather than an administrative one.

Managers approving an expense are confirming that they believe it was necessary for the business and that the amount is reasonable. Managers may not approve their own expenses; those route to the next level of management.

## Submission and Payment

Expenses are submitted through the expense system with receipts attached. Reimbursement is paid through the normal payroll cycle, generally within two pay periods of approval.

Employees should submit expenses in the currency in which they were incurred and let the system apply the conversion rate. Manually converted amounts will be returned for correction.

Employees may not split a single purchase across multiple submissions to stay below an approval threshold. Doing so is treated as a deliberate circumvention of controls.

## Corporate Cards

Corporate cards are issued to employees who travel regularly or make frequent purchases on the company's behalf. A corporate card is for business use only. Personal use, even with prompt repayment, is a policy violation.

Corporate card transactions must be reconciled with receipts within thirty days. Unreconciled transactions after sixty days may result in card suspension. Cash advances are not available on corporate cards.

## Non-Reimbursable Expenses

The following are not reimbursable in any circumstances: alcohol; traffic and parking fines; personal entertainment; in-flight purchases unrelated to work; childcare and pet care; personal grooming; loyalty program fees; travel insurance purchased individually rather than through the company program; and any expense for which the employee has already been reimbursed by another party.

Ordinary commuting between an employee's home and their assigned office is not reimbursable, including on days when the employee attends the office by exception. Travel from an office to a client site during the workday is reimbursable.

## Common Questions

**Can I expense a personal laptop?** No. Personal laptops are not reimbursable without a written exception from both IT and Finance. Use the approved purchasing process to obtain a company-managed device instead.

**I bought a desk chair for my home office. Can I claim it?** Only if you obtained manager pre-approval and documented the business need before the purchase. Reimbursement is not available for a purchase already made without approval.

**My client dinner receipt includes a bottle of wine.** Submit the receipt with the alcohol subtracted from the claimed amount and note the adjustment. Alcohol is not reimbursable, but the rest of the meal is.

**I paid for a conference myself because registration was closing.** Submit it with an explanation. Finance may reimburse where the business need is clear, but pre-approval was required and repeated instances are treated as a compliance issue.

**Is my train ticket to the office reimbursable?** No. Travel between home and your assigned office is ordinary commuting and is not reimbursable.

## Exceptions and Interpretation

Finance owns this policy. Exception requests should be submitted with the business justification and manager endorsement. Exceptions are granted in writing and are specific to the circumstance described.

Deliberate submission of a false or inflated expense claim is a serious matter and may result in termination and recovery of the amounts paid.

# Compensation and Payroll Policy

## Purpose and Scope

This policy describes how compensation is structured, when employees are paid, and how payroll matters are handled at Northwind Systems, a fictional company used for coursework. It applies to all employees in the United States.

Compensation questions specific to an individual, including a person's own salary band or the reason for a particular increase, are handled by People Operations rather than by an automated assistant or a general policy document.

## Pay Structure

Every role is mapped to a job family and a level. Each combination has a salary band with a minimum, midpoint, and maximum, benchmarked annually against market data for the role and the employee's work location.

Placement within a band reflects experience, scope, and sustained performance. New hires typically start between the minimum and midpoint, leaving room for progression without a level change.

An employee at the top of their band cannot progress further within it. Continued growth requires a level change, which is a promotion; see the Performance and Development Policy.

Bands are reviewed annually. Where a band moves and an employee falls below the new minimum, their salary is adjusted to the minimum at the next payroll cycle.

## Pay Schedule

Employees are paid semi-monthly, on the fifteenth and the last day of each month. Where a payday falls on a weekend or a company holiday, payment is made on the preceding business day.

Payslips are available in the self-service portal on the morning of the payday. Employees should review them and report any discrepancy to Payroll within thirty days.

Direct deposit is the standard payment method. Employees must keep their bank details current in the self-service portal; a change made fewer than five business days before a payday may not take effect until the following cycle.

## Overtime and Classification

Roles are classified as exempt or non-exempt in accordance with applicable law. Classification follows the duties of the role, not the employee's preference or job title.

Non-exempt employees are paid overtime for hours worked beyond the applicable threshold, at the rate the law requires. Non-exempt employees must record all hours worked, including work performed outside scheduled hours and work performed remotely.

Managers may not ask or permit a non-exempt employee to work off the clock. An employee asked to do so should report it to People Operations.

Exempt employees are paid a fixed salary and do not receive overtime. Exempt employees on an on-call rotation receive the on-call differential published by Payroll; see the holidays and schedules document.

## Annual Compensation Review

Compensation is reviewed once per year, following the December performance cycle, with changes effective in the first payroll of March.

The review considers performance, position within the band, and any movement in the market benchmark. A Strong rating does not automatically produce an increase, and the absence of an increase in a given year is not a statement that performance was inadequate.

Off-cycle adjustments are made where an employee's scope changes materially mid-year, where a promotion takes effect, or where a benchmark change places an employee below their band minimum. Off-cycle adjustments require People Operations approval.

## Bonus

Employees in bonus-eligible roles have a target bonus expressed as a percentage of base salary, stated in their offer letter.

Payout depends on company performance against the annual plan and on individual performance in the December cycle. The company component is determined after the financial year closes and applies uniformly.

Bonuses are paid in March alongside the annual review. An employee must be employed on the payment date to receive a bonus, except where state law provides otherwise or where the employee left due to a qualifying reason set out in their offer letter.

Employees who joined during the year receive a prorated bonus based on the portion of the year worked. Time on an approved paid leave counts as time worked for this purpose.

## Deductions and Garnishments

Statutory deductions are withheld as required. Benefit premiums, retirement deferrals, and other elected deductions appear individually on the payslip.

Where the company is served with a court-ordered garnishment, Payroll must comply. The employee is notified in writing before the first deduction, and the matter is handled confidentially.

The company does not make deductions for equipment, training, or other costs except where expressly permitted by applicable state law and agreed in writing by the employee.

## Payroll Errors

Where an employee has been underpaid, the correction is made by off-cycle payment as soon as practicable, and in no case later than the next scheduled payday.

Where an employee has been overpaid, Payroll notifies them in writing with the amount and the reason, and agrees a repayment schedule. Repayment terms are subject to the limits of applicable state law, and the company will not recover an overpayment through a single deduction that leaves an employee without adequate pay for a period.

Employees should report a suspected error promptly rather than waiting to see whether it corrects itself. An error identified within thirty days is straightforward to correct; an error identified after a tax year has closed is not.

## Work Location and Pay

Salary bands are benchmarked to the employee's work location. A permanent move to a different location may change the applicable band, and therefore the salary.

Employees planning a move must notify People Operations at least thirty days in advance; see the Remote Work and Location Policy. The notification is required for payroll registration and tax withholding regardless of whether the band changes.

A temporary work location does not change an employee's band. It may, however, create withholding obligations in the temporary state, which is one reason approval is required for stays beyond ten business days.

## Pay Transparency

Employees may discuss their own compensation with anyone. The company does not restrict such discussions and will not take any adverse action because an employee has done so.

Managers may not discuss one employee's compensation with another employee. Salary band ranges for a given job family and level are available from People Operations on request.

## Common Questions

**When am I paid?** Semi-monthly, on the fifteenth and the last day of the month, moving to the preceding business day where that falls on a weekend or holiday.

**My payslip looks wrong.** Contact Payroll within thirty days. Underpayments are corrected by off-cycle payment; overpayments are recovered on an agreed schedule.

**Will I get a raise if I received a Strong rating?** Not automatically. The review weighs performance, your position in the band, and market movement.

**I am moving to another state. Will my salary change?** It may, because bands are benchmarked to work location. Notify People Operations at least thirty days before the move so payroll registration and withholding are correct.

**Can I talk to colleagues about my pay?** Yes. You may discuss your own compensation freely, and the company will not take any adverse action because you did.

**How much does someone at my level earn?** People Operations can give you the band range for your job family and level. Your manager cannot tell you another individual's salary.

## Exceptions and Interpretation

People Operations owns this policy jointly with Payroll and Finance. Individual compensation questions are answered by People Operations. Classification decisions are made by People Operations against the legal standard and are not negotiable with a manager.

# Leave of Absence Policy

## Purpose and Scope

This policy covers leaves of absence at Northwind Systems, a fictional company used for coursework, that fall outside the ordinary paid time off bank. It applies to all regular employees in the United States.

Paid time off covers vacation, personal days, and short illnesses of one or two days. Anything longer, and anything protected by statute, is a leave of absence and is governed by this policy rather than by the Paid Time Off Policy.

Employees should not attempt to determine which category applies on their own. Contact People Operations, describe the situation, and the leave team will identify the applicable leave types. An employee who continues recording PTO for a lengthy absence may unintentionally forfeit stronger protections.

## Types of Leave

| Leave type | Typical duration | Paid | Job protected |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Medical leave | Up to 12 weeks per year | Partly, through disability plan | Yes |
| Family caregiving leave | Up to 12 weeks per year | Partly, where state law provides | Yes |
| Parental leave | 12 weeks | Yes, company paid | Yes |
| Bereavement leave | Up to 5 days per occurrence | Yes | Yes |
| Jury duty | Duration of service | Yes | Yes |
| Military leave | As required by law | Per policy and law | Yes |
| Personal leave | Up to 30 days | No | Case by case |

Entitlements vary by work state. Several states provide paid family and medical leave programmes that are more generous than the company baseline. The leave team applies whichever provides the greater benefit.

## Requesting a Leave

Foreseeable leaves require thirty days' notice where practicable. A birth, an adoption, a scheduled surgery, and a planned course of treatment are all foreseeable.

Unforeseeable leaves should be reported as soon as practicable, generally within two business days of the employee learning that leave is needed. An employee who is hospitalised may have a family member notify the company on their behalf.

Requests go to the leave team in People Operations, not to the manager. The manager is told that an employee will be absent and for how long. The manager is not told the medical reason, and should not ask.

Medical certification is required for medical and caregiving leaves. The leave team sends the certification form and the deadline for returning it. Certification goes to the leave team, never to the manager.

## Parental Leave

Parental leave is twelve weeks at full pay for any parent following a birth, adoption, or foster placement, available to employees regardless of gender and regardless of whether they are the birth parent.

Leave must be taken within twelve months of the birth or placement. It may be taken continuously or, with the leave team's agreement, in up to three separate blocks of at least two weeks each.

A birth parent's parental leave runs in addition to any period of medical leave arising from the birth itself. The medical portion is arranged through the disability plan and does not reduce the twelve weeks of parental leave.

Employees on parental leave continue to accrue PTO and continue to be covered by the medical plans on the same terms as active employment.

## Pay and Benefits During Leave

Whether a leave is paid depends on its type and the employee's work state. Where a state paid-leave programme applies, the company coordinates its own pay with the state benefit so that the employee receives the greater of the two, not both in addition.

Employees may not be required to exhaust PTO before taking a protected leave, and PTO may run concurrently with an unpaid protected leave only where state law permits it. The leave team will explain how this applies to a given situation.

Medical, dental, and vision coverage continues during an approved leave. During an unpaid leave the employee remains responsible for their share of premiums; the leave team arranges collection, and coverage may lapse if payments are not made.

PTO accrual continues during a paid leave. It stops during an unpaid leave lasting more than thirty consecutive calendar days and resumes on return.

## Intermittent and Reduced-Schedule Leave

Where medically necessary, leave may be taken intermittently or as a reduced schedule. Intermittent leave is tracked in hours against the total entitlement.

Employees on intermittent leave should follow their team's normal absence-notification procedure for each occurrence, and should identify the absence as leave-related so it is recorded correctly.

Managers should not require an employee on intermittent leave to explain each occurrence beyond confirming that it relates to the approved leave.

## Returning to Work

The leave team confirms the expected return date and arranges the return. Where a medical leave was taken, a fitness-for-duty certification may be required before return.

An employee returning from a protected leave returns to the same position, or to an equivalent position with equivalent pay, benefits, and working conditions. Equivalence is assessed by People Operations, not by the manager alone.

Where an employee needs an adjustment to their role or schedule on return, they should raise it with People Operations, which will handle it as an accommodation request confidentially.

An employee who cannot return at the end of an approved leave should contact the leave team before the return date. An unexplained failure to return is treated as a resignation only after People Operations has attempted contact and documented it.

## Confidentiality

Medical information supplied in support of a leave is held by the leave team in a file separate from the general employee record. It is not shared with the manager, and it is not used in any performance or compensation decision.

Managers who learn medical details incidentally must not record them, repeat them, or take them into account in any decision.

## Common Questions

**I have been ill for a week and have been recording PTO.** Contact People Operations. An absence of more than two consecutive workdays may qualify as a leave, which carries stronger protections than PTO.

**Does my manager need to approve a medical leave?** No. Leave eligibility is determined by the leave team against the policy and the law. Your manager arranges coverage but does not decide whether you are entitled to leave.

**Will I have to use my PTO first?** Not for a protected leave. Whether PTO runs concurrently depends on your work state, and the leave team will explain what applies.

**Can I take parental leave if I am not the birth parent?** Yes. Twelve weeks at full pay is available to any parent following a birth, adoption, or foster placement.

**Do I keep my health insurance?** Yes. Coverage continues during an approved leave. If the leave is unpaid you remain responsible for your share of the premium.

## Exceptions and Interpretation

The leave team within People Operations owns this policy and is the only function authorised to determine leave eligibility. Where this policy provides less than applicable law requires, the law controls.

COMPANY HOLIDAYS AND WORKING SCHEDULES

PURPOSE AND SCOPE

This document lists the observed company holidays at Northwind Systems, a
fictional company used for coursework, and explains how working schedules,
core hours, and office closures operate. It applies to all employees in the
United States.

Company holidays are paid days on which the company is closed. They are not
deducted from the paid time off bank. A company holiday falling inside an
approved period of paid time off is recorded as a holiday, not as PTO.


OBSERVED HOLIDAYS

The company observes eleven fixed holidays each calendar year:

  New Year's Day
  Martin Luther King Jr. Day
  Presidents Day
  Memorial Day
  Juneteenth
  Independence Day
  Labor Day
  Thanksgiving Day
  Day after Thanksgiving
  Christmas Eve
  Christmas Day

Where a fixed holiday falls on a Saturday, it is observed on the preceding
Friday. Where it falls on a Sunday, it is observed on the following Monday.


FLOATING HOLIDAYS

In addition to the eleven fixed holidays, each employee receives two floating
holidays per calendar year. Floating holidays exist so that employees can
observe days of personal, cultural, or religious significance that are not on
the fixed list.

Floating holidays are requested in the same way as paid time off and require
manager approval, but they are drawn from a separate balance and do not reduce
the PTO bank.

Floating holidays do not carry over. Unused floating holidays are forfeited on
December 31 and are not paid out on separation.

Employees joining after July 1 receive one floating holiday for that calendar
year.


YEAR-END CLOSURE

The company closes between Christmas Day and New Year's Day. The closure is
paid and is not deducted from the PTO bank.

A small on-call rotation covers customer-facing and infrastructure functions
during the closure. Employees who work during the closure receive equivalent
time off in January, arranged with their manager.

The on-call rotation for the closure is published by November 1 so that
employees can plan.


CORE HOURS

Each team publishes core hours during which its members are expected to be
available for meetings and collaboration. Core hours are a window, not a full
working day, and are normally four to five hours long.

Outside core hours, employees manage their own schedule provided their work is
delivered and their calendar accurately reflects their availability.

Employees working from an approved temporary location in a different time zone
remain subject to their team's core hours. Approval of a temporary work
location does not change core hours; see the Remote Work and Location Policy.


ALTERNATIVE AND COMPRESSED SCHEDULES

An employee may request a compressed schedule, such as four longer days per
week, or a shifted schedule that starts and ends earlier or later than the
standard day.

Requests are made to the manager and reviewed against coverage needs. Approved
alternative schedules are recorded in the employee record because they change
how paid time off is calculated: an employee on a compressed schedule records
the hours they were actually scheduled to work, not a flat eight hours.

An approved alternative schedule is reviewed at least annually and may be
adjusted where business needs change, with reasonable notice to the employee.


UNSCHEDULED OFFICE CLOSURES

Where an office closes unexpectedly, for weather, a utility failure, or a
safety incident, the closure is paid and is not deducted from the PTO bank.

Employees who are able to work remotely during an unscheduled closure are
expected to do so unless the closure notice says otherwise.

Closure notices are sent through the emergency notification system, so
employees should keep their contact details current in the self-service
record.


RELIGIOUS OBSERVANCE

An employee who needs time off for religious observance beyond their floating
holidays should raise it with their manager or People Operations. The company
accommodates religious observance where doing so does not create an undue
hardship, using floating holidays, PTO, schedule adjustment, or unpaid time as
appropriate.

These requests are handled by People Operations where an employee prefers not
to discuss the matter with their manager.


ON-CALL AND WEEKEND WORK

Employees in on-call rotations receive the on-call differential published by
Payroll. On-call schedules are published at least two weeks in advance.

An employee who is called out and works during an off-hours period should
record the time. Non-exempt employees are paid for that time in accordance
with applicable law.

Managers should not schedule an employee for on-call duty during that
employee's approved paid time off.


COMMON QUESTIONS

Q: A company holiday falls in the middle of my approved vacation. Does it come
   out of my PTO balance?
A: No. The holiday is recorded as a holiday and only the remaining days are
   deducted from your PTO balance.

Q: Can I carry my unused floating holidays into next year?
A: No. Floating holidays are forfeited on December 31 and are not paid out.

Q: I want to observe a religious holiday not on the fixed list.
A: Use a floating holiday, or speak with People Operations if you need more
   time than your floating holidays cover.

Q: Do I have to take PTO during the year-end closure?
A: No. The closure is paid and does not reduce your PTO balance.

Q: I work a four-day compressed schedule. How much PTO does one day off cost?
A: The hours you were scheduled to work that day. On a four-day schedule of
   ten-hour days, one day off is ten hours, not eight.


EXCEPTIONS AND INTERPRETATION

People Operations owns this document. The holiday calendar is confirmed each
year before open enrolment and published in the self-service portal.

Employees working outside the United States follow the holiday calendar of
their work location, published separately by People Operations.

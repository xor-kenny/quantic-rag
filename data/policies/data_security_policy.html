Data Security and Acceptable Use Policy

Purpose and Scope

This policy defines how information is classified, handled, stored, and shared at Northwind Systems, a fictional company used for coursework, and what constitutes acceptable use of company systems. It applies to every employee, contractor, intern, and vendor with access to company systems or data, on any device, in any location.

Security obligations do not pause outside working hours and do not change when an employee is working remotely, travelling, or on leave.

Where the classification of a document is unclear, treat it as Confidential until the owner confirms otherwise. Under-classifying is a far more common error than over-classifying.

Access and Credentials

Access is granted using least privilege, meaning the minimum required to perform the role and nothing further. Access is reviewed quarterly by system owners, and access no longer needed is removed.

Passwords may not be shared with anyone, including a manager, a member of the IT team, or a colleague covering during an absence. Where someone genuinely needs access during an absence, the correct route is a temporary grant, not a shared credential.

Every employee is issued a password manager licence and is expected to use it. Credentials must not be stored in a browser profile, a spreadsheet, a notes application, a code repository, or a chat message.

Multi-factor authentication is mandatory on all company accounts and may not be disabled. The approved authenticator application on a personal phone is the one permitted use of a personal device for company access.

Devices

Company-managed devices are required for all work. Personal devices may not be used to access company systems, with the single exception noted above for authentication.

Full-disk encryption is enabled on every company device and may not be turned off. Devices lock automatically after five minutes of inactivity. Employees must not disable endpoint monitoring, remove management software, or install software from outside the approved catalogue without IT approval.

Devices must not be left unattended in a vehicle or a public space. When travelling, devices should remain in the employee's possession rather than in checked baggage.

Data Handling

Company data may not be copied to personal cloud storage, personal email, or removable media. Where data must be shared externally, use the approved file-sharing service with an expiry date and, for Confidential or Restricted material, a named recipient rather than a link that anyone can open.

Restricted data may not be downloaded to a local device except where the role requires it and the system owner has approved it. Where local copies are unavoidable, they must be deleted as soon as the task is complete.

Customer personal data may not be used in a test or development environment. Where realistic data is needed for testing, use the synthetic data generator maintained by the platform team.

Printing Confidential or Restricted material is discouraged. Where it is necessary, printed material must be collected immediately and disposed of in a secure shredding bin, never in ordinary waste.

Artificial Intelligence and Third-Party Tools

Company data may only be entered into artificial intelligence tools that appear on the approved tools list maintained by Security. The approved list distinguishes tools cleared for Internal data from those cleared for Confidential data. No tool is currently approved for Restricted data.

Customer personal data, credentials, unreleased financial information, and security findings must never be entered into any external tool regardless of its approval status.

Adopting a new third-party service that will process company data requires a security review before any data is shared, including for a free trial or a proof of concept.

Reporting Incidents

Suspected loss of a device or exposure of company information must be reported to Security immediately, and in any event within twenty-four hours of discovery. Report through the security incident channel or the emergency contact published in the handbook.

Report even when you are not certain something has happened. A suspected phishing email that was clicked, a laptop that briefly went missing and then reappeared, a document sent to the wrong external address, and a credential typed into an unfamiliar login page all warrant a report.

Do not attempt to investigate or remediate an incident yourself. Do not delete the message, wipe the device, or contact the external party. Preserve the evidence and let Security respond.

Employees who report an incident promptly and in good faith will not face disciplinary action for the underlying mistake. Concealment or delay is treated far more seriously than the original error.

Acceptable Use

Company systems are provided for business use. Incidental personal use is permitted where it is limited, does not interfere with work, does not incur cost, and does not involve prohibited content.

Employees may not use company systems to access or distribute unlawful material, to run a personal business, to attempt to circumvent security controls, or to access data they have no business need to see. Curiosity is not a business need.

Employees should assume that activity on company systems may be logged and reviewed where there is a legitimate business or security reason, subject to applicable law.

Common Questions

I clicked a link in a suspicious email. Report it to Security immediately. Do not delete the email. Prompt reporting protects you; delay does not.

Can I forward a document to my personal email to read on the train? No. Use a company-managed device. Copying company data to personal email is prohibited regardless of intent.

Can I paste a customer support ticket into an AI assistant to draft a reply? Only if the tool is on the approved list at the classification of that content, and never if the ticket contains customer personal data.

My colleague is on leave and I need access to their files. Request a temporary access grant through IT. Do not ask them for their password, and do not use a credential they left with you.

I lost my laptop bag on the train and then got it back. Report it anyway. Security will assess whether the device was tampered with.

Exceptions and Interpretation

Security owns this policy. Exceptions require written approval from the Security team, are granted for a stated period, and are recorded in the exception register. No manager may authorise an exception locally.

Violations may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination, and where a violation involves unlawful conduct, referral to the appropriate authorities.

# Paid Time Off Policy

## Purpose and Scope

This policy describes how paid time off (PTO) is earned, requested, approved, and recorded at Northwind Systems, a fictional company used for coursework. It applies to all regular employees in the United States. Contractors, interns engaged through a third-party agency, and workers covered by a separate written agreement are governed by the terms of their engagement rather than by this policy.

PTO at Northwind Systems is a single combined bank. It covers vacation, personal days, and short illnesses of one or two days. It does not cover company holidays, jury duty, bereavement leave, or any leave governed by the Leave of Absence Policy. Employees do not need to state a reason when requesting PTO, and managers should not ask for one.

## Eligibility and Accrual

Regular full-time employees accrue paid time off each pay period. Part-time employees accrue PTO at the published prorated rate. PTO balances are shown in the employee self-service record and may not be advanced without written HR approval.

Accrual begins on the first day of employment and is credited at the close of each semi-monthly pay period. Employees do not accrue PTO during an unpaid leave of absence lasting more than thirty consecutive calendar days.

| Employment type | Scheduled hours | Accrual per pay period | Annual equivalent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Full-time, 0–2 years of service | 40 | 6.15 hours | 20 days |
| Full-time, 3–5 years of service | 40 | 7.69 hours | 25 days |
| Full-time, 6+ years of service | 40 | 9.23 hours | 30 days |
| Part-time | 20–29 | Prorated to schedule | Prorated |

Service tiers change at the start of the pay period following the employee's work anniversary. A change in scheduled hours changes the accrual rate prospectively; it does not adjust hours already earned.

## Request and Approval

Employees should submit planned PTO requests at least five calendar days before the first day away. The employee's manager approves requests based on coverage and business needs. A balance sufficient for the requested hours is required at the time of approval. Unplanned absences should be reported to the manager as soon as practical.

The five-day notice period is a minimum, not a target. For absences longer than five consecutive workdays, employees should give at least three weeks' notice so that coverage can be arranged. Requests spanning a quarter-end close, a scheduled customer launch, or a documented team blackout period may be declined even when submitted well in advance.

Requests are submitted through the self-service portal. A request is not approved until the portal shows an approved status. Verbal agreement, a message in a chat channel, or a manager's informal acknowledgement does not constitute approval.

## Approval Standards for Managers

Managers should respond to a PTO request within three business days of submission. A manager who cannot approve a request should state the business reason and, where possible, propose alternative dates.

Managers may not decline a request solely because a team is busy, and may not require an employee to find their own coverage as a condition of approval. Where two employees request the same dates and only one can be released, the manager should give preference to the request submitted first. If both were submitted on the same day, the manager should consider which employee has taken less time off in the current year.

Managers may not approve a request that exceeds the employee's available balance. Requests that would create a negative balance must be routed to HR, which may approve an advance in writing where circumstances warrant.

## Limits

PTO is recorded in hours. Eight hours normally equals one workday for a full-time employee. Employees should not make non-refundable travel commitments before manager approval. HR resolves exceptions and conflicts with leave laws.

Partial-day PTO is recorded in half-hour increments, with a two-hour minimum for any single entry. Employees working a compressed or non-standard schedule record the hours they were actually scheduled to work, not a flat eight hours.

A company holiday that falls inside an approved PTO period is recorded as a holiday and is not deducted from the PTO balance. A day on which the office is closed for weather or another company-declared reason is likewise not deducted.

## Carryover, Payout, and Separation

Employees may carry over up to forty hours of unused PTO into the next calendar year. Hours above that limit are forfeited on December 31 unless a written HR exception was granted before December 1. Northwind Systems does not buy back unused PTO during employment.

On separation, accrued and unused PTO is paid out with the final paycheck where state law requires it, and otherwise in accordance with the schedule published by the Payroll team. Employees should confirm the treatment applicable to their work state with Payroll rather than relying on the practice of a colleague in a different state.

PTO may not be used to extend a final day of employment. An employee who has given notice may not take PTO during the notice period without written approval from both their manager and HR.

## Interaction with Other Policies

PTO runs concurrently with certain protected leaves where permitted by law; see the Leave of Absence Policy for the governing rules. Where this policy and a protected leave conflict, the protected leave controls.

Employees who become ill for more than two consecutive workdays should contact HR rather than continuing to record PTO, because a longer absence may qualify for a different and more protective category of leave.

Employees working from an approved temporary location outside their home state remain subject to this policy in full. Time spent traveling to a temporary work location is not PTO if the employee is working during the period; see the Remote Work and Location Policy.

## Common Questions

**Can I take PTO before I have accrued it?** Not without written HR approval. The self-service portal blocks requests that exceed the available balance, and managers cannot override that block.

**My manager approved my request verbally. Is that enough?** No. The request must show an approved status in the portal. Ask your manager to complete the approval before you commit to any travel.

**I requested three days but only have twenty hours available.** Three workdays is twenty-four hours for a full-time employee, so the request exceeds your balance and will not be approved automatically. You may reduce the request to two days, or ask HR whether an advance is available.

**Does a sick day come out of PTO?** Yes, for absences of one or two days. Longer illnesses should be raised with HR, because they may fall under the Leave of Absence Policy instead.

## Exceptions and Interpretation

HR owns the interpretation of this policy. Requests for exceptions should be sent to the People Operations team with the business reason and the manager's endorsement. Exceptions are granted in writing and apply only to the situation described in the request.

This policy does not create a contract of employment and may be amended at any time. Where a provision conflicts with applicable state or local law, the law controls and the remainder of the policy stays in effect.
